
# Computer Science ePortfolio

Welcome to my CS 499 Computer Science Capstone ePortfolio. This portfolio highlights the skills and knowledge I have developed throughout my Computer Science program at Southern New Hampshire University.

The projects included in this portfolio demonstrate my experience with software design and engineering, algorithms and data structures, and databases. Each project includes an explanation of the enhancements I completed and the skills I developed throughout the process.

## Professional Self-Assessment

Throughout my time in the Computer Science program at Southern New Hampshire University, I have gained a better understanding of what it takes to develop software and solve problems using technology. When I started the program, I knew I was interested in computers and technology, but I was not completely sure which direction I wanted to take professionally. Over the past two years, I have developed skills in programming, databases, software design, and problem-solving. Completing my ePortfolio has given me an opportunity to look back at my coursework, recognize how much I have learned, and identify areas where I would like to continue improving.

One of my biggest strengths is problem-solving. Throughout the program, I learned that developing software involves more than simply writing code that works. It also requires understanding a problem, considering different solutions, testing the results, and making improvements when something does not work as expected. I encountered these challenges in several courses, particularly when working with unfamiliar programming languages or trying to identify why a program was not behaving correctly. These experiences helped me become more patient when troubleshooting and more comfortable working through problems instead of immediately looking for a different solution.

My experience working full-time in law enforcement while completing my degree has helped me develop skills that I believe will be useful in a technology career. My current position requires me to communicate with coworkers, share information accurately, and work with others in situations where teamwork is important. Although most of my computer science coursework involved individual projects, I understand that developing software in a professional environment often requires collaboration with other developers, stakeholders, and users. My coursework has helped me recognize the importance of understanding requirements, communicating clearly, and considering how technical decisions affect other people. I believe my professional experience, combined with the technical skills I developed throughout the program, will help me adjust to working as part of a technology team.

Throughout my coursework, I also learned the importance of collaboration and communication with stakeholders. Software developers need to understand what users expect from an application and communicate technical information in a way that others can understand. In courses involving software development and project planning, I had to consider user requirements and how design decisions would affect the finished product. My CS-499 code review was another opportunity to practice professional communication. I reviewed existing code, explained its functionality, identified areas for improvement, and discussed the changes I planned to make. This helped me practice explaining technical decisions in an organized way rather than assuming that everyone reviewing my work would already understand the code.

Another important area of growth has been software engineering. In CS-465: Full Stack Development, I worked with Express, Handlebars, MongoDB, and API routes to develop a travel application. This project helped me understand how different parts of a web application work together. I learned how information moves between a database, server-side code, and the pages displayed to users. It also showed me why organizing code into separate components is important when an application becomes more complex. Working through this project gave me additional experience troubleshooting problems and testing functionality as new features were added.

My understanding of data structures and algorithms has also improved throughout the program. I learned that choosing an appropriate data structure can affect how efficiently a program stores, organizes, and retrieves information. Working with structures such as binary search trees and hash-based collections helped me understand that different approaches have different advantages depending on the problem being solved. I also gained experience with recursion, searching, sorting, and input validation. These skills are important because they provide a foundation for developing programs that are both functional and efficient.

Databases have been one of the areas I have enjoyed working with the most. Through projects involving SQLite and MongoDB, I learned how applications store and retrieve information and why database design matters. I gained experience creating database structures, writing queries, connecting applications to databases, and managing the information users interact with. I also learned that changes to a database can affect several parts of an application. This has helped me understand the importance of planning changes carefully and testing them before considering a project complete.

Security is another area that has become increasingly important to me. Throughout the program, I learned that developers need to consider how applications protect information and what could happen if security is overlooked. This includes validating input, protecting user information, controlling access to data, and recognizing weaknesses in existing code. My coursework helped me understand that security should be considered during development rather than treated as something to address only after an application is finished. These lessons have also contributed to my interest in exploring cybersecurity as a possible career direction.

As I approach graduation, my professional goal is to transition out of law enforcement and into the technology industry. I am still exploring which area of computer science I would like to pursue, but I am interested in cybersecurity and remain open to opportunities in IT, programming, and database-related positions. One concern I have is how artificial intelligence is changing the job market, especially for people trying to enter the technology field for the first time. I recognize that finding my first technology position may be challenging, but I also understand that continuing to learn and adapt will be important throughout my career. I have researched certifications such as CompTIA A+, Network+, and Security+ as possible ways to strengthen my knowledge and prepare for future opportunities.

My ePortfolio brings together three projects that demonstrate different areas of my computer science education. For software design and engineering, I selected my C++ graphics application from CS-330: Computational Graphics and Visualization. I enhanced the program by improving the organization of the code and adding interactive functionality. This project demonstrates my ability to work with existing software, make improvements to its design, and add functionality while maintaining the original features.

For algorithms and data structures, I selected my Advising Assistance Program from CS-300. The original application used a binary search tree to organize and display course information. I enhanced it by adding an unordered map for course lookups, recursive prerequisite traversal, circular dependency detection, and additional validation. This project demonstrates my understanding of how different data structures and algorithms can be used together to solve problems and improve an application's functionality.

For databases, I selected my Event Tracker Android application from CS-360. I enhanced the SQLite database by connecting events to individual user accounts, restricting database operations based on user IDs, adding searching and sorting, and improving password storage. This project demonstrates my ability to improve database relationships, manage information more effectively, and consider security when working with user data.

Together, these three artifacts demonstrate my growth in software design, algorithms, databases, and security. They also show how the different areas of computer science connect. A successful application needs organized code, appropriate algorithms and data structures, reliable data management, and consideration for security. Completing these enhancements gave me an opportunity to apply what I learned throughout the program and improve projects that I had previously considered finished.

Overall, completing my degree and developing this ePortfolio has helped me become more confident in my technical abilities while also showing me how much there is still to learn. I know that earning a computer science degree is only the beginning of my professional development. As I work toward entering the technology field, I plan to continue improving my programming skills, gaining practical experience, and exploring opportunities that align with my interests. I believe the projects included in this portfolio provide a starting point for demonstrating what I have learned and how I can continue growing as a computer science professional.

## Databases

For my third enhancement, I selected the Event Tracker Android application that I originally developed in CS 360: Mobile Architect and Programming. The application uses Java and SQLite to manage user accounts and event information.

I enhanced the database by connecting events to individual user accounts and restricting event retrieval, updates, and deletes to the appropriate user. I also added event searching and sorting, improved password storage using hashing, and implemented a database upgrade process.

This enhancement demonstrates my understanding of database relationships, data management, security, and how database changes affect other parts of an application.
